# Claims and substantiation rules (paid social defaults)

## Non-negotiable: avoid absolute claims
Do **not** make absolute, unsubstantiated claims (e.g., "stops ringing ears," "saves lives," "helps you reach REM sleep" without data).

Prefer **open wording**:
- "can help..."
- "helps reduce..."
- "designed to..."
- "made for..."
- "helps you stay..."

## "0% margin of error" claims category
The guidelines flag product-claim language as legally vulnerable; treat as **0% margin of error**. When in doubt, check the legal-approved claims list owned by Insights/Legal.

## Testimonials can carry stronger language (with rules)
Absolute claims are acceptable **inside attributed testimonials**, but you must:
- never invent quotes
- never alter meaning when shortening
- always attribute with a real name/initial and avoid generic signatures
- do not force product names into the quote; add after attribution

## Fear, negativity, and "doom"
You may describe the problem in an informative way, but the product message must land **positive**; avoid scare tactics and insensitive framing.

## Practical workflow for risky claims
When the user requests (or provides) a strong claim:
1) Rephrase to open wording.
2) Flag "needs substantiation/legal-approved claim list."
3) Offer a safer alternative angle (benefit, comfort, returns, reviews).

## Common safe claims observed in real ads
These appear frequently and are considered safe:
- "Award-winning engineering"
- "Certified hearing protection" / "Certified noise reduction"
- "100-day, hassle-free returns"
- "57,000+ 5-star ratings" (verify current number)
- "13M unique customers" (verify current number)
- "22 million+ pairs sold" (verify current number)
- "Reusable and washable"
- "2-year warranty" / "2-year guarantee"
- "Comfortable custom fit"
- dB reduction numbers with (SNR) qualifier, e.g., "16 dB (SNR) of noise reduction"
