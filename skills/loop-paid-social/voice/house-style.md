# House style (paid social defaults)

## Language
- **American English is default.** British English only when explicitly requested for a British audience.
- Use **contractions** to sound conversational (it's, you're, don't).

## Clarity
- Keep it **simple and universal**; avoid tricky/unclear terms and opaque metaphors.
- Avoid colloquialisms/idioms (e.g., "first dibs," "knock your socks off," "taking the mickey").
- If a colloquial term is truly valuable for personality, use inverted commas to show it's intentional.

## Punctuation and structure
- End caption paragraphs with a **full stop**, unless using a colon to introduce a list. End CTA lines with a full stop.
- **Oxford comma**: Always use the Oxford comma.
- **Capitalise after colons**: The word following a colon should be capitalised (US English convention). Example: "Here's the deal: Loop earplugs are made for music lovers."
- **Em dash**: Do not use em dashes in any paid social copy. Use a full stop, comma, or colon instead.
- **No full stops in headlines or descriptions.** These fields are too short for sentence-ending punctuation.
- **Emoji as full stop**: When a line ends with an emoji, do not add a full stop. The emoji acts as the full stop.

## Numbers
- Spell out **one** to **nine**, unless clarity demands figures (e.g., "3 volume modes in 1" or lists). Use figures for 10+.
- Thousands: "57,000" or "57k" (lowercase k). Millions: "22 million" or "22M+" (uppercase M).

## Product names and versions
- Prefer product names without version numbers (e.g., "Loop Quiet"), unless comparing versions or launching a new version.
- Use the trademark symbol (TM) once per caption for the primary product mentioned: "Loop Dream™ earplugs".
- For Switch modes: "Quiet Mode / Experience Mode / Engage Mode" (Mode capitalized in singular).

## Emoji conventions (from real ads)
- Use emojis as visual bullets in benefit lists (one per line).
- Common structural emojis: ⭐️ (features), ✅ (checkmarks), ☁️ (comfort), 🙌 (celebration), 👋 (casual CTA).
- Hook emojis: 😵‍💫 (overwhelm), 😫 (frustration), 🙉 (noise), 😴 (sleep), 🎶 (music).
- Closing emojis: 💥 (impact), 🤩 (excitement), 👇 (CTA pointer).
- Don't overdo it. Emojis should punctuate, not replace, the message.

## Proofing
- Treat spelling/grammar as a "0% margin of error" item; proof twice.
