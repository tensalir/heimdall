# loop-paid-social

Write and QA paid social ad copy for Loop Earplugs, grounded in Loop's performance creative guidelines and a library of 295 proven ads.

## When to use this skill

- Writing new paid social ads (Meta/Facebook/Instagram primary text, headlines, descriptions)
- Creating dynamic product ad (DPA) copy
- Generating ad variations for A/B testing
- QA-ing existing paid social copy against Loop's voice, house style, and compliance rules
- Writing copy for product launches, seasonal campaigns, or brand collabs
- Adapting existing angles for new products or audiences

## Operating principles

1. **Hook -> Relevance -> Trust**: Follow the comms hierarchy (see `frameworks/comms-hierarchy.md`). Every ad captures attention, builds desire, then earns trust.

2. **Simple, bold, human**: Loop's voice is daring, adventurous, authentic. Copy is bold and straightforward with "a bit of zing." Easy for non-native English speakers to understand.

3. **Wit and charm, never mean**: Humor is witty, charming, positive. No cynicism, black humor, or derogatory framing.

4. **Legally safe claims**: Avoid absolute, unsubstantiated claims. Use open wording ("can help...", "designed to..."). Flag anything that needs legal sign-off. See `compliance/claims.md`.

5. **Specific, not generalizing**: When referencing ADHD/autism/neurodivergence, parenting, or kids, avoid judgmental assumptions and absolutes. See `compliance/sensitivities.md`.

6. **Produce variations**: Default to **at least 4 distinct variants** per deliverable unless told otherwise.

## One sharp question rule

If the brief is missing a critical constraint, ask **one** clarifying question. Otherwise, proceed with clear assumptions stated up front.

Critical constraints: product, audience/angle, objective (acquire/retarget/upsell/quiz-funnel/launch), offer/promo, proof points, character limits.

## Inputs (brief format)

Ask for or infer:

- **Product**: Engage / Engage Kids / Experience / Quiet / Quiet Plus / Dream / Switch / Bundles / Mix (multi-product)
- **Audience + angle**: noise sensitivity, sleep, focus, music/nightlife, parenting, travel, fashion, comparison, quiz funnel, generic/brand
- **Objective**: acquire / retarget / upsell / quiz-funnel / launch / seasonal / collab
- **Proof points**: ratings/reviews count, "100-day free returns", "13M unique customers", "22M+ pairs sold", etc.
- **Tone dial**: playful/flirty, confident/practical, calm/soothing, sport/adventure (still recognizably Loop)
- **Hard constraints**: character limits, required phrases, forbidden topics, localization needs, specific campaign (BFCM, gifting, etc.)

## Output contract

For each deliverable, provide:

1. **The copy itself**, clearly labeled by variant, with:
   - Caption (primary text)
   - Headline
   - Description
   - Product assignment
2. **Assumptions** (only if needed)
3. **QA checklist**: Voice + house style + claims + sensitivities pass/fail
4. **Next test ideas**: What to A/B next (hook type, angle, proof point, CTA, emoji style)

### Shared caption default

When variants test different visuals/assets but share the same product and angle, **default to one shared caption** across all variants. Chloe will tweak per variant if needed. Only write separate captions when variants cover genuinely different products, audiences, or angles.

### No repetition of asset copy

The headline, description, and caption must each add something the asset doesn't already say. If the visual/asset already carries a message (e.g., a text overlay), the surrounding copy fields should complement it with broader thematic messaging, product features, or proof points. Never echo or paraphrase the asset's on-screen text in the headline or description. The caption headline should work across all variants in the batch, not mirror any single asset's text.

### Mix/general product matching

Check whether the assets are for a specific product or a general "mix" experiment covering the full range. If the assets are general (showing multiple products or no specific product), the caption must also be general: Mention all products, or none specifically. Do not single out one product in copy when the assets represent the whole lineup.

## Ad anatomy (patterns from the reference library)

These patterns come from analyzing all 295 real Loop ads. They're not rigid rules; they're the gravitational center of what works.

**Caption structure** (avg ~250 chars, range 83-576):
- **Hook line**: question (51% of ads), call-out, confession, micro-drama, or playful identity statement
- **Body**: emoji-bulleted benefit list (43% of ads) OR short narrative paragraph
- **Close**: CTA line + proof point (100-day returns appears in 32% of all ads)

**Emoji usage**: 99% of ads use emojis. They serve as visual bullets, emotional punctuation, and scroll-stoppers. Common patterns: emoji-per-bullet-point lists, emoji at end of hook, emoji in headline. **Keep emoji bullet lists short and snappy:** 3-4 bullets max, each bullet ~5-8 words. These should punch, not explain. **When a line ends with an emoji, do not add a full stop.** The emoji acts as the full stop.

**Headlines** (avg ~31 chars): Short, punchy, often emoji-capped. Functional ("Less noise, more fun") or emotional ("Earplugs your ears will heart"). **Must not repeat the asset's on-screen text.** Instead, go broader on the theme, angle, or product benefit so it works across all variants. **No full stops in headlines.** If the headline ends with an emoji, the emoji acts as the full stop.

**Descriptions** (15-20 chars target): Ultra-short proof points or trust signals. Examples: "100-day free returns", "13M+ customers", "57k+ 5-star ratings", "2-year warranty". This field is tiny in the Meta UI, so aim for roughly 15-20 characters. **No full stops in descriptions.**

**Proof point frequency across all ads**:
- 100-day hassle-free returns: 32%
- Reusable/washable: 15%
- Millions sold/fans: 9%
- Star reviews: 7%
- Warranty/guarantee: 5%

For the full pattern analysis and ad anatomy, see `frameworks/ad-anatomy.md`.

## QA mode

When reviewing existing copy (not generating), check against:

1. **Voice**: bold/simple/human/positive/witty? See `voice/voice.md`
2. **House style**: US English, contractions, Oxford comma, numbers, product names? See `voice/house-style.md`
3. **Claims**: any absolutes without substantiation? Open wording used? See `compliance/claims.md`
4. **Sensitivities**: generalizations about neurodivergence/parenting/kids? Doom-and-gloom? See `compliance/sensitivities.md`
5. **Testimonials**: attributed? Not invented? Meaning preserved? See `compliance/testimonials.md`
6. **Structure**: follows comms hierarchy (hook -> relevance -> trust)? Has CTA?
7. **Consistency**: does it match the patterns in the reference library for this product/angle?

Output a pass/flag/fail per category with specific line-level notes.
